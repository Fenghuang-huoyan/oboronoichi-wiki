The market is the thing **Oboro no Ichi** asks you to run. This page keeps the store’s own headings and the one developer post that explains the setting. It does not add a spirit roster or a sales spreadsheet.

## A market suspended between memories

Under the heading **A Market Between Memories**, the store says:

> Welcome to Oboro no Ichi, a market suspended between memories, where lost souls seek peace. Build, expand, and manage a spiritual market inspired by Japan, welcome wandering spirits, and shape a place that can grow alongside them.

The short description uses the same verbs: build, manage, welcome, produce, sell, and grow the space through memories and a cozy atmosphere. “Inspired by Japan” is the store’s phrase for the look. The header art prints the English title Oboro no Ichi over a lantern-lit stall by the water, with the Japanese line 朧の市 underneath. This site uses the full English name everywhere else so the title stays intact.

The loop that feeds the market is on the store page under **A Simple Yet Deep Gameplay Loop**: organize production, manage sales, expand, and increase appeal so more spirits come. The store calls that cycle relaxing, easy to grasp, and still full of choices. It does not say how appeal is scored.

The calm-down is explicit. **A Calm and Cozy Experience** says there is no rush. Only the slow drifting of spirits, the sound of the wind, and a market that grows alongside them.

## What the 13 May 2026 devlog adds

**Devlog 1 — The Market of Memories** was posted by thelabaofficial on 13 May 2026. It is a personal note, not a systems guide. The parts that describe the market are these:

- Oboro no Ichi is a cozy market-building game set in a spiritual world.
- Wandering souls can rest there, trade away their memories, and slowly find peace.
- The project continues the world of **Midori no Kaori**, with a different meaning. That earlier game, in the post’s words, was about helping lost souls remember and follow their path toward Tokoyo. Oboro no Ichi is about accepting, letting go, and finding comfort in Kakuriyo.
- The work is inspired by peaceful places in Japan, by traditional folklore, and by the idea of games as quiet rooms.
- The developer dedicates it, in part, to people who are no longer here: not as something sad, but as a warm place where memory can still rest.
- The post embeds a YouTube preview (`eesYMsLWXq8`) and asks readers to wishlist the Steam page. It closes with “Thank you for keeping the lanterns lit.”

This site has not watched that video for extra mechanics, so nothing from the footage is treated as a fact here. The written announcement is the source.

<Callout kind="note" title="Two games, one sentence">
Midori no Kaori, Tokoyo, and Kakuriyo appear in that 13 May post. They are not defined further on the Oboro no Ichi store page. This guide does not invent a glossary around them.
</Callout>

## Opening the market

Several Steam screenshots of Oboro no Ichi show the market already built, at night, with lanterns along the paths. A top-center button in those frames reads **Open Market** or **Close Market**, depending on the shot. Small figures stand near stalls in the closer frames. The bar also shows counters. Those numbers change from screenshot to screenshot, and this page does not record them. They are not a published economy.

The store text never defines what opening or closing does, how long a session lasts, or what a spirit buys. The Early Access “current state” answer only says you can welcome spirits inside a loop that is already playable. Sales and appeal are named. A formula is not.

## Appeal, sales, and spirits

What you can say without guessing:

- Spirits are who the market is for. The store says you welcome them, and that higher appeal brings more of them.
- Sales are something you manage, fed by resources you produce and by stalls those resources supply.
- The full version is planned to add new interactions with spirits, and “deeper events and gameplay dynamics during market hours.”

That last phrase is the only time “market hours” appears in the sources checked. It is a promise about the full version, listed beside new items, new structures, and more customization. It is not a timetable, a day-night length, or a list of events.

The developer’s Early Access note says community feedback should keep management depth and the cozy tone in balance. Discord is the channel named in that answer. The other social links on the store page are listed in the footer.
